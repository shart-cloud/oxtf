### Design notes

- **Layout.** Every dispatch enum holds `Box<'a, T>`, so `Expression`,
  `BodyItem`, `TemplateElement`, `TraversalOperator` and `ObjectKey` are all
  16 bytes (tests in `tf_ast/src/lib.rs`). Every node has a `span: Span`
  (two `u32`s). `Atom`s borrow the source unless an escape or a dedent forced
  a copy into the arena.
- **Newlines.** The parser keeps a mode stack. Newlines are significant in
  bodies and in object constructors, where they terminate attributes and items
  (as in Go HCL and hcl-edit). They are skipped inside `( )`, `[ ]`, `${ }`,
  `%{ }` and `for` expressions. A mode is popped *before* the closer is
  consumed, so the token after the closer is seen in the outer mode.
- **Templates.** The lexer has a template mode that scans literal text up to
  `${`, `%{`, `"` or a heredoc terminator line. The parser drives it and
  re-enters normal lexing inside sequences. A quoted string with no
  sequences becomes `Expression::String`; otherwise it becomes `Template`.
  `$${` and `%%{` are decoded. In quoted strings, `\n \r \t \" \\ \uXXXX
  \UXXXXXXXX` are decoded too. The `~` strip markers are recorded as `Strip`
  flags and *not* applied, so the evaluator applies them.
- **`<<-` heredocs** are dedented in the parser using Go HCL's rule
  (`flushHeredocTemplateParts`):
  - Count indentation over line-start literal text, including literals inside
    `%{ }` bodies.
  - Ignore whitespace-only lines.
  - Strip nothing when a `${`/`%{` starts a line.
- **Comments.** `#`, `//` and `/* */` comments go into `ParseResult::comments`
  in source order. On the corpus that is 8,122 comments.
- **Recovery.** Errors never abort parsing.
  - At body level, the parser skips to the end of the line, balancing
    brackets and skipping strings and heredocs as units.
  - In tuples and call arguments, it skips to `,` or the closer, and gives up
    at a `}` or when a new line starts with `ident =`, `ident {` or
    `ident "`. That way a missing `]` or `)` does not swallow the rest of the
    file.
  - In objects, it skips to the end of the item.
  - Inside `${`, a missing `}` resumes literal scanning at the offending
    token.
  - A missing expression becomes `Expression::Error`.

## Q2: Visitor codegen, build-time crate or proc macros?

**Choice: build-time codegen crate (`tf_ast_codegen`, syn + prettyplease)
with committed output.** This is oxc's `oxc_ast_tools` model. The generator
reads `ast.rs` and classifies each type:
- a struct with a `span` field is a node;
- an enum with data-carrying variants is a dispatch enum;
- anything else is a leaf.

It emits `AstKind`/`AstType`, `Visit`, `VisitMut` and `GetSpan`: 1,818 lines
generated from 392 lines of `ast.rs`. `tests/fresh.rs` and `--check` fail on
stale output.

To get evidence rather than opinion, I built the other approach as a
prototype that reuses the *same* generator library:
- `tf_ast_macros::generate_visitors!("ast.rs")`, a function-like proc macro;
- `tf_ast_pm`, which compiles the same `ast.rs` through that macro.

Both are in commit `daba104` and were removed in `a8fc883` to keep syn off
the default build. I measured them at the real AST size (42 types) and on a
synthetic AST 10 times larger (420 types, the real `ast.rs` copied with
renamed types; 21,421 lines including generated code). The scaled run is
outside the workspace. Dev profile, 4-core Xeon @ 2.80GHz. The other W2
agent was building in the same container, so the timings carry noise; three
runs of each are shown.

Reading the evidence:
- **Clean and CI builds:** the proc macro adds about 4 to 5 s to every cold
  build of `tf_ast` and of every crate downstream of it. Syn sits on the
  critical path of the whole workspace.
- **Codegen's cost:** a one-time generator build of about 6 s, paid only by
  whoever changes the AST.
- **Edit-and-iterate loop:** a wash. At 10x, the proc macro saves the
  separate 0.5 to 0.7 s generator invocation (a debug build of the
  generator), but the totals overlap within noise.
- **Not measured, but visible in the tree:** with codegen, the generated
  `visit.rs` is greppable, reviewable in diffs, and go-to-definition works in
  rust-analyzer without macro expansion.

So codegen keeps compile times better for everyone and iteration speed
equal. That is the same trade oxc made.

The cost of using hcl-edit is not "zero parser". We still need an AST of our
own:
- hcl-edit's tree is owned (`Vec`/`String` per node);
- its spans are `Option<Range<usize>>`;
- it collapses duplicate object keys;
- it has no recovery.

So the hcl-edit route is "our AST plus a converter". The closest measured
proxy for that converter is the 279-line hcl-edit walker in the differential
test. A real converter would also allocate into the arena, so it would be
somewhat larger.
- Owning the parser costs about 1,550 more code lines (1,826 versus about
  279).
- Agent wall time per git timestamps: the AST and codegen landed at 17:29:56
  and the lexer and parser at 17:36:02 (`git log`). That reflects agent time,
  not human-engineer time.

|---|---|---|
| returns a tree | 12/12 | 0/12 (no recovery; `Err` only) |
| valid attributes recovered | 24/24 | 0/24 |

Per-case table: see the appendix. Caveat: for "missing `}`",

(missing `]` and missing `)`) are reported where the mismatch is detected,
not at the opener.

have not optimised this; it is dominated by boxing every node.

### Verdict on Q1

Owning the parser is worth it. It costs about 1.8k lines of parser code and
buys three things:
1. **Recovery.** We get a tree for every broken file. hcl-edit returns
   nothing, which matters for the LSP-adjacent future and for scanning
   repositories with one bad file.
2. **About 6.5 to 7.4x throughput** straight into the arena AST we need
   anyway.
3. **Control over HCL corner cases** where hcl-edit deviates from Go HCL:
   duplicate keys, nested heredoc dedent and multi-line quoted strings
   (below).

hcl-edit stays valuable as the differential oracle.

## Corpus results

- **Parse:** 2192 files (2169 regular `.tf` + 5 `.tfvars` + 18 `.hcl`:
  `.tftest.hcl`, packer, tflint), 6,670,971 bytes, **0 files with errors**.
  Two `.tf` paths in `Azure__terraform-azurerm-aks/unit-test-fixture` are
  symlinks and are not followed.
- **Differential:** 2192 files, **2192 identical**, 0 diverged, 0 rejected by
  hcl-edit, over 6,680,855 bytes of normalised S-expressions.

**Normalisation** (`tf_parser::dump` with `normalize: true`, mirrored for
hcl-edit in `tests/common/mod.rs`). It drops trivia, comments and spans, and
additionally:
1. merges adjacent template literals;
2. applies `~` strip markers to neighbouring literals (idempotent, so it does
   not matter whether either side pre-applied them);
3. folds `-<number>` into a negative number (hcl-edit does this in its
   parser; tf_parser keeps `Unary(Negate, 1)` like Go HCL);
4. omits the `<<-` marker (hcl-edit only records an indent when it stripped
   something);
5. collapses duplicate object keys (`dedupe_object_keys`, see below).

**Divergences found and how each was resolved:**

1. **Duplicate object keys: hcl-edit is lossy.** hcl-edit's `Object` is a
   `VecMap`, so a repeated key keeps its first position and takes the last
   value. The corpus has this in 4 files. For example,
   `terraform-google-network/examples/global-network-firewall-policy/main.tf:265-266`:
   ```hcl
   match = {
     dest_ip_ranges    = ["10.100.0.102/32"]
     dest_ip_ranges    = ["10.100.0.2/32"]
   ```
   tf_parser keeps both items, which is better for a security analyzer since
   it can flag the shadowed value. Before the normalisation, the differential
   showed `tf_parser: (dest_ip_ranges (tuple "10.100.0.102/32"))` versus
   `hcl-edit: (dest_ip_ranges (tuple "10.100.0.2/32"))`. The same applies to
   `terraform-aws-alb/examples/{complete-alb,mutual-auth-alb}/main.tf`, where
   `port = 80` appears twice in the `ex-instance` target group. The
   differential emulates hcl-edit's map semantics for comparison only. The
   tf_parser tree is unchanged.
2. **`<<-` heredoc with literals inside `%{ }` directives: hcl-edit is
   incomplete.** hcl-edit's `Template::dedent` only dedents top-level
   literals. Go HCL dedents the flat token list. Not in the corpus. It is
   pinned as a known divergence in `snapshots.rs`
   (`heredoc_indented_directive`). Example input:
   `<<-EOT\n  %{ for s in var.subnets ~}\n  subnet ${s}\n  %{ endfor ~}\n  EOT`.
   hcl-edit leaves `"\n  "` before `%{ endfor }`; tf_parser yields `"\n"`.
3. **Multi-line quoted strings: hcl-edit accepts, tf_parser rejects** (with
   recovery), matching Terraform's "Invalid multi-line string" error. Pinned
   in `snapshots.rs::multiline_quoted_string`. Not in the corpus, since
   tf_parser reports 0 errors there.

While building the differential I found no tf_parser bugs on the corpus; it
matched on the first full run, apart from item 1. One bug in my *test input*
(a quoted template containing literal newlines) was caught and rewritten.

## Scope cuts (and why)

- **No miette integration for `Diagnostic`.** It is a plain
  `{span, message}`. Adding `miette::Diagnostic` is mechanical and belongs
  with W4's renderer.
- **No lossless CST or trivia.** Formatting and autofix are non-goals.
- **The arena footprint (4.22 B/B) was not optimised.** Candidates are
  unboxed small literals (with a non-16-byte `Expression` trade-off) and
  interning. The criterion only fixes `Expression` at 16 bytes.
- **The proc-macro prototype was removed after measurement.** It is kept in
  history at `daba104`.
- **Recovery is heuristic.** There is no indentation-aware block recovery
  (see the "missing `}`" caveat).

## Known gaps

- **Identifiers** use `char::is_alphabetic` / `is_alphanumeric` plus `_`/`-`,
  not exact UAX#31 `ID_Start`/`ID_Continue`.
- **`<<-` dedent** counts `char`s; Go counts grapheme clusters. This only
  differs for combining characters in indentation.
- **Numbers:** `NumberLiteral::value` is `f64`. The raw text is kept in
  `raw` for integers beyond 2^53.
- **Strip markers** are recorded but not applied in the tree. The evaluator
  (W3) must apply them; `tf_parser::dump::normalize_template` shows the rule.
- **`Expression::String` versus `Expression::Template`:** consumers must
  handle both for string values. `Expression::as_string_literal()` covers the
  first.
- **Heredoc closing delimiter** lines may be indented for both `<<` and
  `<<-`.
- **tf_allocator clippy warning:** `cargo clippy` reports one warning in the
  pre-existing `tf_allocator::Box::as_ref` (not W1 code). tf_ast, tf_parser
  and tf_ast_codegen are clippy-clean with `--all-targets`.
